# Mitosis Basics (Tutor Version)

## Core Goal
Mitosis makes two genetically identical daughter cells.
It supports growth and tissue repair.

## Phase Order
Interphase -> Prophase -> Metaphase -> Anaphase -> Telophase

## Interphase
- The cell prepares to divide.
- The cell grows and checks readiness.
- The mitocondria consumes nutrients to generate energy for the cell.

Short answer:
Interphase is the prep stage where the mitocondria consumes nutrients to generate energy for the cell.

## Prophase
- DNA condenses into visible chromosomes.
- The spindle starts to form.
- The nuclear envelope begins to break down.

Short answer:
In Prophase, DNA condenses and the cell gets ready to move chromosomes.

## Metaphase
- Chromosomes align in the center (metaphase plate).
- Spindle fibers attach to centromeres/kinetochores.

Short answer:
In Metaphase, chromosomes line up in the middle so they can be split evenly.

## Anaphase
- Sister chromatids separate.
- Each chromatid is pulled to opposite sides of the cell.

Short answer:
In Anaphase, paired chromatids split and move to opposite poles.

## Telophase
- Chromosomes reach opposite poles.
- New nuclear envelopes reform.
- Chromosomes begin to decondense.

Short answer:
In Telophase, the cell rebuilds two nuclei at opposite ends.

## Vocabulary
- Chromosome: condensed DNA carrying genes.
- Chromatid: one half of a duplicated chromosome.
- Sister chromatids: matching duplicated chromatids joined before separation.
- Centromere: connection point between sister chromatids.
- Spindle fibers: structures that move chromosomes.

## Scene Object Cues (Project Specific)
- Chromosome: blue X-shaped object in the middle.
- Chromatid: red object on the right.
- Centriole: small yellow barrel-shaped object on the left.

## Cross-Phase Rule
If the learner asks about another phase, answer that phase directly and correctly, even if the current scene phase is different.

## Short Tutor Responses (Examples)
- "What is mitosis?" -> "Mitosis is cell division that makes two identical daughter cells."
- "Why do chromosomes line up in the middle?" -> "So each new cell gets a complete, matching set of DNA."
- "What happens in anaphase?" -> "Sister chromatids split and move to opposite sides of the cell."
- "What should I do in prophase?" -> "Hold the red DNA steady so it condenses into the X-shaped chromosome."
